Open the **Timeline**. Above the canvasser table there's a **by-team** breakdown: each team's people, doors, survey doors, surveys taken and connection rate — plus a **Campaign** row underneath.

That last row matters. The teams **add up to the campaign total**, so you can read one team's number to a client and know it's consistent with the number you'd quote for the whole campaign. Click any team to drill into just their canvassers.

Everything on this page is about **one campaign**. Crews are set per campaign, so the same canvasser can be on Dana's crew here and on Luis's crew in another campaign at the same time — and each campaign's table shows only its own split.

## What "doors" means

**One door = one house, in one round.** If two canvassers knock the same house in the same round, that's **one** door, not two — the second knock was almost certainly a mistake, and you shouldn't bill for it. If you deliberately go back in a **new round**, that's a new door.

This is why the "Doors" number is a little lower than the raw count of everything everyone recorded, and the line under the table explains the difference whenever there is one.

## People who left the team

**Their doors still count, and they still count toward their team.** Somebody who knocked for you and then quit — whose account you deactivated, who you removed from the campaign or the organization, or whose account was deleted, by them or by an admin — keeps their doors on the team they were on until they're added back. You don't have to do anything.

**Losing a person never moves a number.** Two things do, both on purpose: changing somebody's crew, and someone coming back to the campaign — see below.

**If a coordinator's account is deleted,** their team keeps every door it already has, and it's still labelled with their name for 180 days — in the by-team table and the Coordinator column alike — then *Deleted user*. The people on their crew have no coordinator until you set one on the campaign's **Team** tab, and when you do, their doors on that campaign move to the new team, as below.

If you ever want to *change* a departed person's team: a **deactivated** account that's still on the campaign is fine — open the campaign's **Team** tab and set their crew as usual. Somebody you **removed from the campaign** isn't on that Team tab anymore; add them back and pick their crew while you do, and their earlier doors there move to it (or to No team if you pick none). That's the same for every role, and it's also what happens when a book puts an admin or a team lead back on the team.

## Moving someone to another team

You set a person's crew in one place: open the campaign, go to its **Team** tab, click them, and pick their crew in their panel — or tick several people and use **Move to crew…**. (The **Users** page just lists which crew they're on in each campaign, with a link through to the campaign that sets it.)

**All of their doors in that campaign move with them,** including the ones they knocked before the change. Their doors in your other campaigns don't budge — a crew change here is not a crew change everywhere.

That's what makes a forgotten setup step fixable: if you add a canvasser to a campaign, forget to put them on a crew, and they knock all day, you can set their coordinator afterwards and every one of those doors moves onto that team. You're not stuck with a pile in "No coordinator".

The flip side is worth knowing: **a team number you quoted last month can change if you reassign somebody afterwards, or someone who worked here before comes back** — though only in the campaign it happens in. Before a crew change saves, the app tells you exactly how many of *this campaign's* doors will move and from which teams. Changed your mind? Set the crew back and the numbers return exactly as they were. A change that was cut off partway shows **Finish it** on the person's panel.

Two things a team change never touches:

- **The campaign's own totals**, coverage, and your connection rates. Only the split *between* teams changes.
- **Your bill.** Billing counts doors, not teams.

One case that surprises people: if you give a coordinator to someone who **runs a crew themselves**, their *own* doors in that campaign move onto their new coordinator's team. Their crew's doors stay with them. The confirmation says so before you commit.

Another one, for the same reason: **running a crew in one campaign doesn't make you a coordinator everywhere.** If a lead runs a crew here but simply knocks doors in another campaign — nobody reporting to them there, and no coordinator of their own — their doors in that campaign sit under "No coordinator". That's right, not a glitch. Give them a crew or a coordinator on that campaign's Team tab if you'd rather see them counted somewhere else.

## "Knocked by two different teams"

If you ever see that note under the by-team table, two teams have worked some of the same houses. Each team is credited for the work they actually did, and the campaign still counts the house once — so the numbers stay honest. But it usually means two walk lists overlapped, and it's worth a look.

## Team client reports don't add up the same way

A **client report** for a team counts differently from this table. Each team report counts that team's own work, so a home both teams visited counts in **both** team reports (each report says so), and the team reports **don't add up to the campaign total**. The whole-campaign report counts that home once, like the **Campaign** row here. Team reports also leave out doors marked at the office, and never show a door goal. See [How do team reports work?](how-do-team-reports-work)

## Surveys: doors vs voters

You'll see **two** survey numbers, because they answer different questions:

- **Survey doors** — houses where at least one survey was taken. This is what the **connection rate** divides by.
- **Surveys taken** — how many survey forms were filled out. One house can have several voters, so this is usually higher than survey doors. Survey the same person again in a later round and that's another survey — see [Why do I see different survey numbers?](two-survey-numbers).

Neither is wrong; they're just counting different things.
