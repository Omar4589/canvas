They look similar in the field but don't all count the same. The question that separates them is **how far you got** — and, when someone answers, **who** it was:

- **Refused** — you reached the door **and a person**. Someone came out and turned you away. This **is** a knock and **is** a contact (you reached someone). A refusal is recorded only with this button on the door screen — never with a survey answer, even one named Refused.
- **Not a target voter** — you reached the door **and a person**, but not one of the voters on your list for that address, and they wouldn't give their name (someone who does give a name can be added with **＋ Add person** and surveyed instead, unless your campaign has limited adding people to team leads and admins). It counts exactly like Refused — it **is** a knock and **is** a contact, and it is **never** a survey. It's an optional button, only on survey campaigns that have it turned on. If you don't see **Not a target voter**, record the door as you do today and mention it to your lead.
- **No soliciting** — you reached the door, but a posted sign ended the visit and nobody answered. This **is** a knock — you made the same walk as any other house — but it is **not** a contact, so it doesn't lift your "reached a person" rate.
- **Restricted access** — you never reached the door at all: a gated community, a locked building, no access. It's fully visible and counted as its own category, but it is **not** counted as a knock (you never actually knocked).

All four stay on the map and in your counts. An admin can also mark a home restricted **from the desk** — a whole book or a single house — which looks the same on the map but is nobody's work and is never billed. On your phone that home says **Marked restricted by the office**: it means someone expects the door to be unreachable, not that you're barred from it. If you can reach it, work it normally — what you record replaces the mark. For **restricted** and **no-soliciting** homes, an admin can choose to **exclude** them from the next round's books so nobody is sent back — while leaving the option to try again later if access opens up or the campaign changes its mind. The two toggles are separate, so you can drop one, both, or neither.

## Billing

A **no-soliciting** door bills like any other knock, automatically — no setting to turn on, because the canvasser's walk was identical to a not-home. A **Not a target voter** door bills the same way a refusal does: it's a knock.

A **restricted** home is never a knock, so by default it isn't billed at all. If you invoice a client per door, an admin can choose to count it as a **billable door**, since the canvasser still made the trip. That's a separate setting, decided by an admin, and it doesn't change any of the above.

## Should we knock past a no-soliciting sign?

That's your campaign's decision, not something the app decides for you. Political canvassing is generally **exempt** from no-soliciting ordinances — it's protected speech rather than commercial soliciting — so many campaigns do knock. Others honor the signs as a courtesy. Ask your organizer which your campaign does; either way, recording the sign is what lets your admins skip those homes in a later round.
