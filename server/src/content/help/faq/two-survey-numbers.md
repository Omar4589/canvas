Because they're counting two different things, and both are right.

- **Survey doors** — *houses* where at least one survey was taken.
- **Surveyed voters** — *people* surveyed.

A single house can have several voters living in it. Survey two people at one door and that's **one survey door** but **two surveyed voters**. So Surveyed voters is normally the higher number, and the gap tells you how deeply your canvassers are working each house.

A canvasser showing **18 survey doors and 37 surveyed voters** is averaging two voters per house — that's someone taking their time at the door, not a bug.

## Which one does the connection rate use?

**Survey doors.** The connection rate is *"of the doors we knocked, how many completed the goal"* — so both sides of that fraction are doors. If you try to check the maths with the voter number instead, you'll get a different percentage and think something's broken.

## Do they ever add up?

**No — never add them together.** They're two lenses on the same work, not two parts of a total.
