**It works the same whether they deleted it themselves or an admin did.** An org admin can delete an
account that belongs only to your organization — see
[How do I delete someone's account?](delete-an-account)

**Your numbers don't move.** Every door they knocked and every survey they recorded stays with the
campaign. Those are your organization's records of work performed, not the person's personal content, so
deleting an account never changes a campaign's totals, its coverage, or your bill. Nothing needs
recalculating. If your organization connected **FbTime**, the hours they clocked stay with their account
too, so their doors-per-hour stays measured.

**Their name stays for 180 days on the screens you report and audit with.** Doorline keeps it privately,
so these still show who they were:

- canvasser reports, on the web and in the app;
- team labels and by-team totals;
- the GPS audit, including its flags on the map;
- Survey Results downloads and every other export.

**Other screens show "Deleted user" right away:** the rest of the map, a door's history, survey details
and other detail screens. Their rows there are all still present, with the right numbers.

**You can still audit them.** This is the important one. Their GPS trail stays intact, and for **180 days**
the GPS audit still names the real person. **Nobody can delete their way out of a quality audit** — if you
flagged their doors, the flags and the coordinates survive, and you can still see whose they were. That
holds even if you'd deactivated them first, and in any organization where they'd recorded doors or
surveys before. After 180 days their name is removed for good; the records stay in your campaign history
but no longer directly identify them, and every screen reads *Deleted user*. That includes the Door
Outcomes change list: a change you filtered by their name reads *Deleted user* there from then on.

**Their books came back to you.** Any walk lists or books they were holding are automatically **unassigned**
when the account is deleted, so no doors are stranded with someone who's gone. Books they shared stay with
the other canvassers on them. Reassign the rest as normal. They're also removed from every campaign roster.

**If they coordinated a crew**, those people have no coordinator until you set one on the campaign's
**Team** tab. The team keeps its numbers, and shows their name for the same 180 days.

**Doors left on their phone.** Someone deleting their own account has to let the app send everything
first. When an admin deletes it, anything still on their phone goes up under whoever signs in on that phone
next (until a phone update after Election Day).

**Your Users list says what happened.** A used account's row stays as **Deleted account**, with who deleted
it and when — *Deleted their own account*, *Deleted Oct 23, 2026 by Alex Kim*, or *Deleted through
Doorline support*. When an admin deletes an account nobody ever used, it disappears from the list instead.

**They can't be restored.** Deletion is permanent by design — you can't set a temporary password to bring
them back, and you can't put them back on a roster. If they return, add them as a new person: their email
address is released when the account is deleted. They'll get a **brand-new account**, and their old knocks
stay attached to the deleted one.

**If your organization uses FbTime**, keep them as one person there: on the Integrations page, unlink the
old account and choose **Yes — keep**, then link the new account to the same FbTime person. Their old hours
stay with the old account, so last season's doors-per-hour stays measured, and if you send door counts to
FbTime, both accounts' doors are added together for that person. See
[FbTime: measured hours and door counts](fbtime-hours).

**Admins have guardrails.** Nobody can delete the organization's **only admin**, or its **only admin with
billing access** — not the person themselves, and not another admin. That would leave the organization
with nobody who can run it or pay for it. The app blocks it and says to hand off first.
