## When the house is the one asking

A resident tells a canvasser: *never come to this door again.* Not "don't talk to me" — don't come
here at all.

[Do not contact](do-not-contact) can't honor that on its own. It's a fact about a **person**, so
the door only disappears once every registered voter there is flagged — and until then canvassers
are still routed to that address for the housemates. But the knock IS the contact: you can't ring
the bell for a housemate without summoning whoever asked to be left alone.

**Do not knock** attaches the request to the **address** instead.

## Who can mark an address

**Org admins, team leads, and super admins.** A team lead can mark any door in a campaign they
manage.

**Canvassers cannot.** The request usually reaches us at the door, but marking an address suppresses
it across your whole organization, permanently — so it's a decision a lead or admin makes. Tell your
canvassers: record it in the door note, then tell you.

## How to mark one

Two places, both needing a written reason:

- **Map** — click the door, then **Mark do not knock…** in the panel.
- **Voter profile** — in the **Household & campaign** section.

To see every suppressed address, go to **Voters → Do-not-knock addresses**.

## What changes

- The door leaves turf cutting, books, walk packets and every canvasser's map **immediately** —
  including books already cut and already downloaded to a phone.
- It applies in **every campaign**, now and in the future. If the same address is in two campaigns,
  both doors go dark at once — the confirmation tells you how many.
- It's counted in reports as its own **Do not knock** coverage segment instead of sitting in
  "unknocked".
- **Nobody is flagged personally.** The residents keep their own Do-not-contact status, so someone
  who moves away carries no mark from a request about a house they left.
- Nothing historical changes. Knocks already walked stay billed, past surveys stay in reports.
- Admins still see the door on the admin map, badged — that map is the record of work performed.

## It does not reopen on its own

This is the one place Do not knock deliberately behaves the opposite way to Do not contact.

A fully-flagged Do-not-contact door **reopens** when a new resident is imported, because that person
never asked for anything. A Do-not-knock address **never** does — the request was about the house,
and a refreshed voter file showing new names is not consent.

Because households really do turn over eventually, the register flags any suppressed address that
has gained a voter imported *since* the request, so you can review it. Nothing reopens until you
lift it yourself.

## Two things to know about matching

- **Addresses are matched exactly.** If two campaigns' voter files write the same house differently
  ("123 N Main St" vs "123 North Main Street"), only the one you marked is suppressed. The register
  has a **Check similar addresses** link that finds these so you can mark them too. We never
  auto-match, because a coincidence could darken a neighbor's door with nobody noticing.
- **Suppression survives a campaign delete.** Delete the campaign and re-import that address years
  later — it comes back already suppressed.

## Lifting it

Only an admin or lead can lift a request, from the same door panel, or from **Voters →
Do-not-knock addresses** for an address whose campaign no longer exists. The address becomes
knockable again everywhere at once.
