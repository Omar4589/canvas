## What a client report is

It's the update you used to put together by hand and email: headline numbers, a voter-contact breakdown, your support and survey breakdowns, your written observations, and a map of where the team has been. You build it for a period, check the preview, and publish it. **Each published report has its own link**: your client opens it, types the password you gave them, and reads that report. There's no account for them to create. Next week's report gets its own link, which can keep the same password, so you send a password only once (see [Why does each report have its own link?](share-just-one-report)).

**A published report is frozen.** The team can keep knocking and late answers can arrive; the report doesn't move. Its numbers change only when you press **Refresh numbers**, and the report then says "Numbers refreshed" with the date. For newer numbers, make the next report and share its link.

## The page's guide

Under the **Client Reports** title, the page says what it's for: "Make reports for your client here. Each one shows the campaign's running totals and what was added in the dates you pick. Share it with a link and its password, or download a PDF." The first time you open it, a short guide, **How client reports work**, sits at the top of the reports list:

1. **Make a report** — press New report and pick the dates, usually Last week. After that, Next report on your newest report makes the following one in one click, with your settings and notes copied.
2. **Check it, then publish** — the preview is exactly what your client will see. Once it's published, its numbers stay as they are until you press Refresh numbers.
3. **Share it** — Share makes the report's link and a message to paste into your email or text. Next week's link keeps this week's password, so you send a password only the first time, or after you change it. Or open the report and press Download PDF.

**Got it** hides the guide for you on this browser; **How it works**, at the end of the page's description, brings it back.

## What your client sees

- **A password screen** that shows nothing about the campaign until the password is right. **Keep me signed in on this device** keeps them signed in for up to 30 days; left unticked, it lasts until the tab closes. Once in, **Sign out on this device** is always there.
- **Your logo** at the top, if you've added one, with a small "Powered by Doorline" at the foot.
- **The report itself, straight away.** A report's link opens only that report: there's no list of other reports and no way to page to another week, and the page says "This link opens this report only."
- **Each report** reads top to bottom: the headline cards with "+412 this week" (or "+812 in these 14 days" on a two-week report), door-goal progress if you ticked it, the voter-contact breakdown, the support breakdown, voter groups, survey breakdowns, your observations, and the map. It shows when it was published and last edited or refreshed, as dates.
- **Every number runs from the campaign's first door through the report's last day**, and the report says so under **Activity at a glance**: "Totals since the campaign began, through Oct 25." Only the "+" figures are the report's own dates. See [Does a report count only the dates I pick?](report-totals-and-dates)
- **A Latest progress report** reads "All time" above its dates and "Totals since the campaign began, as of Oct 28." ("through Oct 28" once its numbers are refreshed after that day), with no "+" figures anywhere, on the page, the list and the PDF.
- **Download PDF** gives them the same report as a PDF, with your logo and without the map.
- **If you take the report down**, its link says "This report was taken down or removed. Ask the person who sent it." until you put it back up. If you delete the report, its link is deleted with it and says "This link isn't available. It may have been replaced or switched off — ask the person who sent it."
- **An old link to all your reports** (the kind made before each report had its own link) opens a list of every report instead, with tabs for the whole campaign, each team and each walk list, until Doorline turns those links off. See *Old links to all your reports* below.

The **voter-contact breakdown** puts every door knocked into exactly one outcome — *Surveyed*, *Declined to participate*, *Didn't answer*, and so on — so it always adds up to Doors knocked. On a campaign that uses the optional **Not a target voter** door button (an org admin has to turn it on), it gains a **Not a target voter** row, explained to your client as: *"Doors where someone answered, but it wasn't one of the voters on our list for that address and they didn't share their details. We reached a person there, just not a voter we could survey."* It appears only in a report that has at least one such door.

The **map** shows only the doors the team reached, colored by how each one went, with **no canvasser names or locations**. Your client can filter it by result and by the questions you put on the map. On a phone it fills the width, moves with two fingers, and shows a tapped door's address and answers below the map. Several doors at one spot, like an apartment building, show their results one by one, never with apartment numbers. A tap that catches several neighbouring spots at once (easy when the map is zoomed out) reads "12 doors near here — zoom in to pick one" instead of calling them one building.

The map keeps up with the report. When your client comes back to the tab, or opens the report again after a minute, and you've refreshed its numbers, switched it between exact doors and areas, or added or taken off a map question, the map reloads with it.

Your client can also use the map **from the keyboard**: Tab to it, move it with the arrow keys and zoom with + and −. A red ring marks the centre; **Enter** shows the doors inside it (or the area under it) and **Escape** closes them again. A screen reader explains this and reads out what was opened.

A report can also carry a short note when it applies: **"Numbers as of Oct 24 — the period was still running"** when you built it before the period ended, and **"Totals from before Oct 19 were updated by −120 doors after they were first reported."** when the previous report's totals changed after you sent it (a late knock, an edited answer, a canvasser moved between teams). That line never says why, and the preview shows it the same way. If you refresh the earlier report's numbers after this one was built, the line disappears rather than compare two different sets of numbers; building this report's numbers again compares afresh. On a Latest progress report it names the earlier report's last day instead: **"Totals through Oct 25 were updated by −120 doors after they were first reported."** (On an old link to all your reports, while it still works, the same line names the earlier report: "Earlier totals were updated by −120 doors since the Oct 13 – Oct 19 report.")

## Build a report

1. Open the campaign and go to its **Client Reports** tab, then press **New report**.
2. Choose what it **covers**: the **Whole campaign**, **A team**, and optionally **Only one walk list**. A walk list's name shows on your client's report, so a colorful internal name is worth a second look.
3. Pick the **dates**. A line above them says what they decide: "Your report shows totals for Paid team, from the campaign's first door through the last day you pick. The first day only decides what shows as new, like “+412 this week”." (it names the whole campaign, or your team or walk list). The choices are listed with their dates, the first one picked: what comes after the tab's last report, then **Latest progress · all time, through today** (see *Latest progress* below), then **Last week**, then **This week, not over yet** (refresh it once the week ends), then **Pick dates** for anything else. Leave Pick dates' first day empty to count everything up to the last day. They're worked out fresh each time you open New report ("Loading the dates…" for a moment), so a page left open overnight still suggests today's dates. Dates that haven't begun say so: "Starts Oct 26 — its numbers stay empty until then." See *Report dates* below.
4. Press **Create draft**. The numbers build in the background ("Building the first numbers…"), and you can leave the page while they do.

If the tab already has a report for exactly those dates, New report asks first, right under the dates, for example "You already have a draft Whole campaign report for Oct 19 – Oct 25." with **Open the draft** or **Make a second one** ("Each one gets its own link once it's published."). For a published one it says how to bring its numbers up to date: open it and press **Refresh numbers**. For one you've taken down, it says "The taken-down one stays hidden until you put it back up. The new one gets its own link once it's published." For one you've archived, it says so ("— it's archived.") and offers **Unarchive**, which puts it back in your list. **Make a second one** makes exactly what you pressed **Create draft** for. If someone else pressed **Create draft** for the same dates at the same moment, only one draft is made, and you read "Ada Lovelace made the Whole campaign report for Oct 19 – Oct 25 a moment ago — you're both in it." with **Open it**.

## Report dates

Report dates follow **your organization's report week**, Monday to Sunday unless an admin changes it. The first choice in **New report** is what comes after the tab's last report:

- a report that ran **whole weeks** is followed by the same number of weeks, on the same weekday: "Last week · Oct 19 – Oct 25 — the week after your last report", or "Oct 26 – Nov 8 · 2 weeks — the two weeks after your last report";
- **whole months** are followed by the same number of months: "November — the month after your last report";
- a report of **any other length** is followed by a short **catch-up** to the end of last week, so the one after it is a normal week: "Oct 14 – Oct 25 · 12 days — from the day after your last report to the end of last week, so your next report covers a full Monday – Sunday week".

With no report in the tab yet, the first choice is **Last week** (or **This week, not over yet** for a campaign whose first doors were knocked this week). After a long gap it is the most recent full week or month instead, and a line names the days no report covers, for example "No report covers Oct 12 – Oct 18."

Under the choices, admins read "Report weeks run Monday – Sunday for your organization. Change" and can pick another weekday there; it changes the dates new reports suggest, for every campaign, and reports you've already made keep their dates. Team leads read who sets it: "(set by an admin)".

If a tab's weeks run on another weekday than your organization's (most tabs made before this started on whatever day their first report was made), New report says so, for example "Your last Whole campaign report ran Wednesday – Tuesday. Your organization's report weeks run Monday – Sunday." **Move this tab to Monday – Sunday weeks** picks a short catch-up first, after which the tab runs Monday to Sunday; an admin can instead **Use Wednesday – Tuesday weeks for the whole organization**.

In the builder, the left side is your settings and the right side is the **preview, exactly what your client will see**, updated as you go. Changes save on their own.

- **Title** and **Canvasser observations**: sections with a heading and a paragraph each.
- **Survey questions**: one row per question, with a **Chart**, **Map** and **Headline** box. **None** really means none: a question whose chart you hide can't go on the map or be the headline. It can still be hinted at elsewhere: a ticked voter group can count its Yes answers, and a follow-up's chart shows how many people took the path through it. **Before you publish** warns you when a voter group or a follow-up chart hints at a question you've hidden.
- **Voter groups** (your survey tags) are **hidden until you tick them**, so an internal label like "Hostile" can't reach a client's page by accident. A ticked group shows as *Supporter — 400 identified · 380 still current*.
- **Door goal**: tick **Show door-goal progress to the client** to show the campaign's door target as of that period. It's **off unless you tick it**, it shows progress only (no pace, no "behind", no finish date), and it isn't offered on a walk-list or team report.
- **Map**: **Show the map**, then **Exact doors** or **Areas** (see below).

**Follow-up questions go on the map only with the question they follow.** Say "Could we put a yard sign up?" is asked only after a Yes to "Back the recall?". If the yard-sign answers were on the map, anyone could see which homes said Yes to the recall, even with that question hidden. So the builder keeps the follow-up off the map unless "Back the recall?" is on it too, and says why. Usually it's best to leave the follow-up off the map; its chart is still your call.

The header shows **Numbers as of** with a time, and **Recompute** builds the numbers again from today's records. If someone is fixing answers, changing or removing door entries, or re-cutting a round on the campaign, the build waits for them to finish and says so. Changing the map after the numbers were built (turning it on, adding a map question, switching between exact doors and areas) needs a Recompute before you can publish; taking something off never does.

## Latest progress

When your client asks "how many doors have we knocked? how many surveys? how much support?", make a **Latest progress** report: in **New report**, pick **Latest progress · all time, through today**. It counts everything from the campaign's first door through today, so it has no "+412 this week" figures. Pick it for a team (say your paid team) and it's everything that team has done.

- It starts on the day of the campaign's first door, so it shows dates like "Sep 14 – Oct 28", and your reports list says "All time · Sep 14 – Oct 28".
- For everything up to an earlier day, use **Pick dates** with the first day left empty.
- It sits at the top of your reports list, since reports are listed by their last day, newest first.
- Your weekly reports aren't affected: their suggested dates, Next report and the "not covered by any report" lines ignore Latest progress reports.
- Its link shows the totals through its last day, like any report's. Doing it every week? Press **Next report** on your newest Latest progress report: it makes a new one through today with your settings and notes copied, with its own link to send (Share offers the same password).
- Before you publish, it reminds you that "Today isn't over yet: doors knocked after these numbers were built aren't in them."

## Before you publish

The builder lists what's worth a look before the report goes out. Nothing here stops you publishing:

- open **mock-location flags** in this report's doors, with a link to review them in the [GPS audit](audit);
- knocks that arrived while the numbers were being built, or numbers more than an hour old (press **Recompute**);
- a period that isn't over yet;
- a follow-up chart, or a voter group, that hints at a question you've hidden;
- answers given under an earlier version of your survey, from before Doorline kept a record of its follow-up questions, when a chart is hidden and the map is on, so you can check your map questions;
- notes copied from last week that you haven't reviewed;
- an earlier report whose totals changed since you sent it, and why (or, if that report's numbers were refreshed after these were built, that the "earlier totals" line is hidden, with **Recompute** to compare again);
- on a team report, **Door visits this period** (on a Latest progress team report, just how many canvassers have knocked for the team since the campaign began, with the same one-person warning): a row for each team, this report's team first, and "Together" when there are two or more; then the door visits in the whole campaign (or the walk list) this period, how many of them more than one team made (they count in each team's report), and how many no team made (canvassers with no crew in a team, or doors entered in bulk); and how many canvassers knocked for the team (it warns when it's one, since the report then shows one person's work);
- how many surveys an admin edited after they were recorded, or entered at the office instead of at a door. They count like any other answer.

None of this is ever shown to your client. Nothing here is about links: a draft never has one, and Share makes the report's link once it's published.

## Publish

Press **Publish…**. Under its title the confirm says what happens next: "Oct 19 – Oct 25 — after you publish, Share makes its link for your client." (while an old link to all your reports still works, it says that link shows the report as soon as you publish). It repeats anything important and says "Your client sees exactly the preview." **Publishing freezes exactly what your screen shows, at once.** Pressing it while you're still typing waits for your last change to save. If the report or its numbers changed somewhere else since your screen last read them, nothing is published: the page and the preview update and say why, so you can look again and press Publish once more, with no reload.

**If someone else works on the same report while you have it open**, nothing either of you saves overwrites the other. Every save checks that the report is still the version your screen shows; if it changed elsewhere, the builder merges the two and sends only your changes:

- with nothing of yours unsaved, your screen simply updates and says what changed;
- with your own unsaved changes in other places, both are kept;
- **lists of choices never clash**: which charts, map questions and voter groups show are merged one by one, so a chart someone else hid stays hidden unless you tick that very chart again;
- **observation sections** are matched by their content, so a section either of you added is kept and one you deleted goes;
- when you both changed the same thing (the title, the headline question, the map or its view, the door goal, or the same section), a yellow banner names only that and asks you to choose **Keep mine**, which says exactly what it replaces (for example "their title “Week 3 final”"), or **Use theirs**. Anything else either of you changed is kept;
- if they publish the draft you're editing, your change never goes to your client on its own: it opens as an unsent edit, kept on this device, for you to review and send with **Update report**.

**If you leave the page mid-change**, the last change is still sent on the way out, and a copy is kept on this device first. If it didn't land (say someone changed the report in that moment), the draft offers it back next time: "A change you made didn't save before you left." **Restore it** puts it back on top of whatever the report holds now; **discard it** throws it away. If the report was published meanwhile, it comes back as **Restore your unsent edit** instead.

> Tip: Closing the tab with a change still waiting to save only makes the browser ask first; it doesn't save it. Give it a second, or press **Publish…**, which waits for the save.

Once it's published, the builder says "Published — Share makes its link for your client." with **Share**.

## Share: each report's link

Every published report has a **Share** button, on its row and at the top of the report, and every report has **one link of its own**. Share makes that link the first time, and after that always shows the same link. Your client's link opens only that report, never another, and next week's report never appears on it. Share reads your links first ("Loading links…"); if they can't be loaded, it says "Couldn't load the links." with **Try again**.

**The first time**, under "Make this report's link", pick the password:

- **Same as your last link** is picked for you when this tab already has a link whose password Doorline kept: "Same as your last link (Paid team · Oct 12 – Oct 18)". Your client keeps one password all season. When only another tab has one, it's offered but not picked, since that tab may go to someone else.
- **Make one up for me** makes a 12-character password.
- **Type my own** takes 12 or more characters. Under it: "Everyone who manages this campaign can see this password later. Don't use one you use anywhere else."

Then pick how long it lasts (90 days unless you pick 30, 180 or 365) and press **Create link**. There's no label to type. A password made up or typed shows right there, "Its password: …", with **Copy password**, until you press **Done**.

**After that**, Share shows the link's address with **Copy link**, its password as dots with **Show** (then **Copy password**), **Change password**, when it expires and when it was last opened, with **Extend** (or **Renew** once it has expired), **New address** and **Delete link**, and **Unlock now** when it's locked. A link that's turned off (only an older one can be) also has **Turn on**; there's no Turn off, because **Take down** does that job for one report.

**Copy message** gives you a message to paste into your own email or text, with the report's dates, its headline numbers and its link. It never includes the password, and it ends by what you just did: "— I'll send you its password separately" after you made up or typed one, "— I'll send you the new password separately" right after a change, and "— use the password I sent you" when you chose Same as …, or open Share again later.

**Seeing the password again.** Org admins and the campaign's team leads can press **Show** any time. A link made before Doorline kept passwords says "This link's password can't be shown. To have one you can see, change it — then send your client the new password." See [I lost a client link's password](lost-client-link-password).

**Change password** makes one up or takes one you type. When other links of the campaign use the same password, it offers to change those too, already ticked: "Also change it on the 3 other links that use this password (Whole campaign, Paid team)". Changing a password signs out anyone using the old one, so send your client the new one. **When someone who manages the campaign leaves, change the password**, because they still know it.

**Doorline staff can't see or type link passwords.** Working in your organization under a support session, staff can make a link (with a password made up for them, or Same as …) or change a password by having one made up, but they never see a password and can't type one in.

**A report you've taken down** keeps Share, so you can manage its link, but its message can't be copied until you put the report back up. **A report that got two links before each report had its own** keeps both; Share shows the second one under the first, "Also opens this report: Board · made Oct 25", with **Delete**.

## Change a report after it's published

A published report never goes back to being a draft. Instead:

- **Edit** fixes the title, the notes, which charts and voter groups show, and the headline question, and can take questions or the whole map off. Press **Update report**; your client sees it right away, marked "Edited". Update sends only what you changed since you pressed Edit, so a change someone else made meanwhile stays. **The numbers don't change.** Team leads on the campaign can still read the earlier text in History. Turning the map off deletes the frozen map but remembers which map questions you'd picked; bringing it back means refreshing the numbers.
- **Your edit is kept on this device** until you press **Update report** or **Cancel edit**. Leave with it unsent and **Restore your unsent edit** offers it back next time; pressing **Edit** opens it too. **Cancel edit** asks before it throws your changes away.
- **Refresh numbers** rebuilds the report from today's records, for the same dates, and shows you **Now** and **After** side by side. Nothing changes for your client until you press **Apply**; **Discard** throws the new numbers away. It's also how you add to the map: turn it on, add a question, go back to exact doors, or change the area size. **Turning the map back on** offers the map questions the report had picked, already ticked; untick any you don't want, and the review says which questions will be on the map before you apply. Your client sees "Numbers refreshed".
- **Take down** hides the report from your client but keeps it exactly as it was. **Put back up** shows it again, unchanged, with its original publish date. When the report has a link, the confirm says "Its link shows “taken down” until you put it back up."
- **Delete** removes it for good, and its link with it. If your client can still open it, the warning says so ("Your client can open the Oct 19 – Oct 25 report right now.") and suggests Take down instead. Anyone who opens a deleted report's link reads "This link isn't available."

> Tip: Fixed a canvasser's wrong survey answers after a report went out? The published report keeps the old answers until you open it and press **Refresh numbers**, then **Apply**.

**Worth a look.** Once a report is published, a small **Worth a look** box keeps the checks that can still matter: a map question that shows no answers by area, answers from an older version of your survey, and an earlier report whose numbers were refreshed after this one was built (with **Refresh numbers** to compare again). It appears only when one applies, and your client never sees it.

## Your reports list and Archive

Each report's row shows its dates, whether it's a draft, published or taken down, its headline numbers, and two short lines: how its link is doing ("Link: active · expires Jan 25", "No link of its own yet — Share makes one.", "Link expired Jan 25 — Share to renew it.", "Link locked after 50 wrong passwords — Share to unlock it.") and how often it was opened ("About 5 visits · your organization: 2 visits"). Every action is a button: **Open**, **Share** and **Next report** on the left; **Archive**, **Take down** (or **Put back up**) and **Delete** on the right. On a phone they sit on two lines.

**Archive** tidies your list and nothing else: your client's link keeps working. The report moves into a folded **Archived** list at the bottom of its tab, and the page says "Archived — it's under Archived (4) at the bottom. Undo". **Unarchive** brings it back. Drafts can be archived too.

- The newest report of each tab (and its newest Latest progress report) can't be archived, because that's where **Next report** lives. If it ends up archived anyway (say, a newer one was deleted), it stays in the list, marked Archived.
- An archived report still counts for its dates: New report knows those dates are taken.
- Opened, an archived report says "Archived — it's out of your list. Nothing changes for your client." with **Unarchive**.

## Next report

On the newest report of each tab, **Next report** makes the next draft in one click, starting the day after the last one ended and following the same rules as New report's first choice (see *Report dates*). It copies your notes (marked as copied until you tick **I've reviewed the copied notes**), your question choices, the map, your voter groups and the goal tick, and starts building the numbers right away. Pressed twice, it still makes one draft.

On a **Latest progress** report, Next report makes a new one through today instead, with the same settings and notes; it isn't offered on one that already runs through today (press **Refresh numbers** on that one). Your weekly reports keep their own Next report.

After a long gap, Next report fills the gap from the day after your last report, while New report suggests the most recent full week. One click never makes a catch-up longer than two weeks, though: Next report then opens **New report** with **Last week** picked, and the top of its dates says "More than two weeks have passed since this report. Choose the next report's dates in New report."

## Team reports

If your client wants separate reports for a volunteer team and a paid team, make a **team**: a name your client sees plus one or more crews. "Paid team" can be Sam's crew and Dee's crew together. Each crew can be in only one team, and canvassers with no crew lead aren't in any team's report.

- **A team name can't include a canvasser's name**, because your client reads it. A walk list that happens to share a name with one ("Marco Island") gets a warning you can confirm.
- **Each team report counts that team's own work.** A home both teams visited counts in each team's report, and the report says so, so team reports don't add up to the whole campaign. Team reports never show a door goal.
- **Edit team** renames a team, changes its crews, or ends it. Its published reports stay exactly as they were sent. Ending it can also delete its open drafts; only reports that are still drafts go, and each one is recorded in History.
- **Continue this team** brings an ended team back: its tab offers it instead of Edit team. Pick the crews it covers from now on (a crew you leave unticked leaves the team); its published reports stay exactly as they were sent. Picking an ended team in **New report** offers **Continue the team** first, and afterwards New report reopens on that team with your dates and title kept.

If a crew lead leaves the campaign, the team's next draft stops and asks what to do. By default her crew's past doors **stay in the team** while those canvassers have no crew; you can also remove them (the card says how many doors the team's totals would drop, up to the end of that report), change the team's crews, or end the team. Moving one of those canvassers onto another crew moves their past doors with them, and on the web the confirm that moves them names the client report whose numbers change (the phone doesn't show that line yet).

## Exact doors or areas

**Exact doors** shows each home your team reached, with its street address when tapped. **Areas** shows shaded hexagons instead, small (about 1,100–1,400 ft across) or large (about 0.55–0.7 mi across), each with how many doors were reached and how they went. **Areas never show an address.**

In area view, a single-choice question shows its answers area by area only when **every** area has at least five answers to it; otherwise your client sees just that question's overall chart. Large areas usually fix that. Even so, an area where every home gave the same answer tells your client how each of them answered, and other reports your client can open can narrow things down. The hexagons are see-through so the streets show, and the legend's colours match them as they look on the map.

On a published report, **Switch to areas** is part of Edit: your client keeps seeing exact doors until the areas are ready. Its confirm names any map question that may end up with no answers by area, and once the areas are up, **Worth a look** says which ones did. Going back to exact doors means refreshing the numbers.

## Old links to all your reports

Before each report had its own link, a link could open **every** report of a campaign, and each new report appeared on it the moment it was published. No new ones can be made. **Doorline is turning them off for everyone**; once it does, anyone who opens one reads "This link no longer works. Ask the person who sent it for the link to the latest report."

While your campaign has any, they're listed above your reports under **Old links to all your reports**:

- While they still work, each row says what the link does now, for example "Candidate · still opens every report · last opened Oct 26", with **Change password** and **New address**, and every row has **Delete**. Deleting one that still works asks first: "Your client loses every report on it now, not on the day Doorline turns these off. Send them each report's own link first. This can't be undone."
- Once Doorline has turned them off, the card says so ("Old links to all your reports — turned off"), and **Delete** just tidies them away.

They can't be turned back on, renewed or extended. **To move a client over**, on each tab they follow, press **Share** on the newest published report and send its link. To keep the password your client already has, choose **Type my own** and type it (it needs 12 or more characters; a shorter one can't be kept, so make up a new one and send it); on that client's other tabs, choose **Same as** that link.

## Wrong passwords

Share shows a link's wrong passwords for the last day, the week and in total, whenever there are any, even on a locked or expired link. No link accepts more than **50 wrong passwords in any 24 hours**: at 50 it locks, though anyone already signed in keeps reading. Fifty wrong passwords is never your client, so Share suggests **New address**. **Unlock now** lets the right password work again straight away. A link whose password turns out to be shorter than 12 characters is flagged so you can change it.

## Who opened it

Each published report's row says how often it was opened, for example "About 5 visits · your organization: 2 visits". It counts every visit the report ever had, through any link. A visit is counted once per browser per day, so treat it as an estimate. Visits by people signed in to your organization are counted separately, so checking your own work doesn't inflate your client's number. **Last opened** on a link is the last time someone signed in to it opened it.

## Your logo

An org admin can put your organization's logo on every client report and PDF from the **Report branding** card: **Upload** takes any common image, and the app turns it into a clean, small picture first. Removing it removes it from every campaign's reports. Team leads see the logo but can't change it.

## History

**History** on the Client Reports tab lists every publish, edit, refresh, take-down, archive and delete, every team change, and every link change, with who did it and when. Each row names its report's tab or its team, so "Oct 12 – Oct 18, 2026 · Paid team report · Published" and "Volunteer team · Team continued" read apart from the rest. A link's rows name its report the same way, for example "Oct 12 – Oct 18, 2026 · Paid team report · Link created". A password changed on several links at once says so on each. A link deleted with its report shows "Deleted with its report" under it. Showing a password isn't recorded. It doesn't appear in the campaign's general History or in the phone app. Team leads on the campaign can read it, including the earlier text of edits, and can **Restore** an earlier text. An org admin can **Erase** that text for good.
