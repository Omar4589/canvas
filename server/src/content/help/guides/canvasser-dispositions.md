When you tap a house, you record what happened. Your pin recolors instantly so you always know what's done.

## The app needs your location

Every door you record carries a GPS stamp — it's how your work gets verified — so **the app won't record a door without one**. If your phone's location is off, or Doorline doesn't have permission (or only has your *approximate* location on iPhone), the tap is blocked with a message telling you exactly what to turn on, plus a shortcut to Settings and a **Try again** button. Nothing is saved until location works. A red notice also shows at the top of the map whenever location is off, so you'll know before you start walking. See [Why does Doorline need my location?](why-location-required) for the details.

## The outcomes

- **Survey / talked to them** — you reached someone and (on a survey campaign) took the survey. This is the goal.
- **Lit dropped** — on a lit-drop campaign, you left literature at the door. This is the goal there.
- **Not home** — nobody answered. You may come back later.
- **Wrong address** — the house isn't right (vacant, demolished, not a residence).
- **Refused** — someone came to the door and declined to talk. You still reached a person.
- **Not a target voter** — only if your campaign has it turned on (survey campaigns only). Someone answered who isn't one of the voters on your list for that address, and they won't give their name. You still reached a person — it counts like Refused, never as a survey.
- **No soliciting** — the door had a posted no-soliciting sign and you honored it. You still reached the door, so this counts as a knock.
- **Restricted access** — you physically couldn't get to the door: a gated community, a locked building, no way in.

## Who answered the door?

On a survey campaign, what you tap depends on who answers the door and what they'll share:

- **A voter on your list takes the survey** — tap **Take survey** on their card.
- **A voter on your list declines** — tap **Refused**.
- **Someone not on your list gives their name** — tap **＋ Add person**, then survey them. See [Someone answered who isn't on my list — can I add them?](add-a-person-at-the-door).
- **Someone not on your list won't give their name** — tap **Not a target voter**, if your campaign has it turned on.
- **Nobody answers** — tap **Not home**.

Already surveyed someone at this door? Then the door's result is done for this round — keep surveying anyone else there, but if someone else declines, don't tap **Refused** or any other door button (the app warns you if you try).

On a lit-drop campaign you just tap **Lit dropped**.

## If a button is missing

Your campaign can turn some outcomes off — if you don't see, say, **No soliciting**, that's a choice your organizer made, not a bug. **Not home** and the goal outcome (Survey / Lit dropped) are always there. **Not a target voter** works the other way round: it stays off unless your campaign turns it on, so you may never see it. If you don't see **Not a target voter**, record the door as you do today and mention it to your lead.

When your campaign changes which buttons you get, an up-to-date app picks up the change on its own within about 30 seconds while you're on the map. If your phone is offline, it keeps the buttons it has until it's back online, then picks up the change the same way — no refresh needed. In the rare case the setting changes right as you tap, the app tells you the outcome is turned off, nothing is recorded, and the button disappears. A door you recorded while your phone was offline still syncs normally, even if the setting changed in the meantime — your work is never thrown away.

## Refused vs. No soliciting vs. Restricted

Three outcomes, no conversation, and they mean different things — the question is **how far you got**.

- **Refused** — you reached the door *and* a person. Someone answered and turned you away. That's a real contact.
- **No soliciting** — you reached the door but nobody answered; a sign ended the visit. It counts as a knock (you made the walk), but not as reaching anyone.
- **Restricted access** — you never reached the door at all. A gate, a locked lobby. Use this **only** when access is the problem.

See [Refused vs. Restricted access](restricted-vs-refused).

> Not sure whether to knock past a no-soliciting sign? That's your campaign's call, not the app's — ask your organizer. Whichever way they decide, recording the sign lets your admins skip those homes next round.

## Changing a door's result

You keep one result per door per round, so recording a different one replaces your earlier result. When a door already shows a result this round — yours or a teammate's — and you tap a different one, the app asks first: **"Change this door's result?"** Tap **Record** to go ahead, or **Cancel** to leave the door as it was. If the change would delete a survey you took at that door, you get a stronger warning instead, **"You surveyed someone here"** — tap **Keep my survey** unless you really mean to delete those answers. A fresh door, or the same result again, is still one tap. See [Why does the app ask before I change a door's result?](why-does-the-app-ask-before-changing).

> Tip: Made a mistake? Just re-open the house and record the correct outcome — your latest choice wins.
